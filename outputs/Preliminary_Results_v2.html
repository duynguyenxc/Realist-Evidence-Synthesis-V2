2Why relations were 0/40 exact, and what human normalization gives (your question 2)

The three automatic levels were: pattern 39/40 (97.5%), anchored 22/40 (55%), exact 0/40. The reason exact was 0 is specific and fixable, not a failure of recall:

  • Our relations are extracted inside a single CMOC, and each gold concept is matched to one representative extracted concept. Two concepts Richmond links often sit in different CMOCs, so a direct edge between exactly those two representatives almost never occurs.
  • In fact, both endpoints were independently recovered for 32 of the 40 relations — the concepts are there; only the exact edge alignment is missing.

When we normalize concepts to the granularity Richmond actually analyses at (grouping our concepts into the broader concept families, which is what a human reviewer does), the exact count rises from 0 to 15/40 (38%) at the same predicate, 16/40 if a differing predicate is allowed. Completing the full human normalization (mapping every extracted concept to its Richmond concept and reconnecting across CMOCs) is expected to move this further toward the 22–32 range. This is the immediate next task, and the worksheets for it are attached (human_validation_relations.csv, human_validation_concepts.csv).

3Independent human validation (your question 3)

We prepared two worksheets so an expert can validate every item, with a blank verdict column for exact / partial / missing:

  • human_validation_concepts.csv — the 47 concepts, each with Richmond's wording and location, the system's match and its evidence quote, and the automatic status (same-role, cross-role, or missing).
  • human_validation_relations.csv — the 40 relations, each with its pattern / anchored / family-exact status and whether both concepts were found.

For inter-rater agreement, the same sheets can be given to two raters and compared. The programme-theory scores are currently model-judged (see §4) and should be rated by an expert before any published claim.
