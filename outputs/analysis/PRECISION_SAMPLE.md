# Adjudicating the concepts where spurious extraction would hide

Generated by `scripts/precision_sample.py` from `outputs/analysis/singles_adjudication.json`.

## What was adjudicated, and why these

Verification has only ever measured recall against the 46 concepts in the gold standard. Precision has never been measured. Of the system's 183 concepts, 24 are the ones the gold entities map onto and 159 lie outside that frame — which is not an error, since the review reported what it chose to report.

Inside those 159 there is one subset where spurious extraction would show up if it existed: concepts resting on a **single mention in a single study**. There are **79**, and all 79 were adjudicated one at a time against their own verbatim quotes.

> **Note on the count.** This was previously reported as 72. That figure used the pre-correction set of 31 matched concepts; with the 24 that survive the matcher fix of 2026-11-04, the correct number is 79. Several gold entities map onto the same system concept, which is why 31 gold matches do not give 31 distinct concepts.

**Who judged.** Claude, in conversation with the RA — not the pipeline, and not a human coder. The extractor is a model of the same family, so self-preference is possible, and this project has already measured judges disagreeing by 0.31 on identical inputs. **Treat what follows as a triage that says where a human should look, not as a measurement.**

Read that against what it is. This is the hardest subset of the output, chosen precisely because single-instance concepts are where extraction noise would collect. A defect rate of 16% *here* implies a lower one across all 183, though how much lower is not something this sample can say.

Of the 66 correct extractions, **40 are concepts that could do analytic work across studies** and **26 name something specific to one study**. The second group is the reason precision as a single number was the wrong target: a concept naming one study's stimulus set is extracted correctly and cannot be used, and neither 'hit' nor 'error' describes it.

### `role` — Wrong type, or label contradicts its quote (4)

**`cont:diagnostic-back-tracing`** · Context · S009

> trace back through a cascade of data abnormalities, using logic to connect them to underlying mechanisms of disease

A reasoning process typed as a Context. It is what the learner DOES, so it belongs under mechanism.

**`inte:tacit-pick-up-approach`** · Intervention · S028

> clinical reasoning should not be considered as something tacit that students simply ‘pick up as they go along’

The quote REJECTS this approach: 'clinical reasoning should not be considered as something tacit'. Extracting it as an intervention inverts the source.

**`outc:case-familiarity-effects`** · Outcome · S020

> However, this positive effect was only demonstrated with unfamiliar clinical cases.

A moderation finding — 'only demonstrated with unfamiliar cases' — typed as an Outcome. It is a context condition on an outcome, which is precisely what a CMOC's C slot is for.

**`outc:lower-accuracy-with-explicit-instruction`** · Outcome · S005

> Those in the combined reasoning condition signiﬁcantly outperformed those in the spontaneous reasoning condition

Checked against S005's full text, which separates a 'combined reasoning condition' (instructed) from a 'spontaneous reasoning condition' (no instruction). The EXTRACTION is right: the instance label reads 'lower accuracy THAN explicit combined instruction', which is what the quote supports. The CANONICAL NAME is wrong -- normalisation renamed the concept 'lower accuracy WITH explicit instruction', which asserts the opposite. The defect is in the naming step, not the extractor, and it is only visible by reading the source paper.

## Five of them are in Richmond's own paper

The 159 concepts "outside the frame" are outside the **coded gold standard**, which was built from the five CMOC statements and figures. That is not the same as being outside **Richmond's paper**, and the difference matters, because the review says plainly why its frame is narrow:

> "The selected studies identified key contexts for clinical reasoning interventions at the individual level, with contexts at the teacher or the wider organisation level rarely discussed. Therefore, the results presented below predominantly focus on individual student level contexts."

Reading the original paper (`data/paper-Richmond-original.pdf`) against these 79 concepts, **5 are discussed in Richmond's own analysis** while being absent from the gold standard:

- **`inte:reasoning-process-instruction`** — reasoning-process instruction
  Richmond, Context 2: "When an expert's reasoning processes or thoughts are explicitly revealed and discussed (Mresource) with students with sufficient domain-specific knowledge (C), this promotes understanding"
- **`inte:test-enhanced-case-presentation`** — test-enhanced case presentation
  Richmond, Discussion: "Retrieval practice, interleaving and spaced practice are examples of learning strategies, which induce 'desirable difficulties' amongst learners"
- **`mech:immediate-feedback`** — spaced immediate feedback
  Richmond, Discussion: "...also refinement after receiving feedback from teachers following performance on tasks" and "spaced practice are examples of learning strategies"
- **`mech:real-case-practice`** — practice with real patient EHR cases
  Richmond, Discussion: "This research identified that exposure to real patients in practice or simulated environments was necessary for making 'safe mistakes'"
- **`outc:diagnostic-reasoning-confidence`** — increased diagnostic reasoning confidence
  Richmond, Results: "Key student level factors influencing the effectiveness of interventions included an individual's self-confidence, self-efficacy and pre-existing level of knowledge"

**How that figure was arrived at, including the part that was wrong.** A word-level overlap check first returned 19 of 79. Nineteen was contaminated: the matches were coming from Richmond's **reference list**, which contains the titles of the 28 included papers, so a concept named after one study's intervention matched its own citation. Restricting the search to the body text gave 12. Of those, six carried the claim, and each was then checked by locating an actual sentence — five held and one, `mech:self-explanation-examples`, did not: its words occur separately in Richmond's prose but nothing there discusses self-explanation examples. It is recorded in the data as a false positive rather than dropped. **19 to 12 to 5.** An automated overlap count would have reported the first number.

What this changes: the system is not inventing concepts Richmond's authors would disown. For at least five, it recovered something the reviewers themselves wrote about and did not formalise into a CMOC. That is a finding about the **benchmark's** coverage, not only about the system's precision.

## What follows from this

**The five `over_split` verdicts have a single cause, and it is now fixed.** `_batched_cluster` clusters labels forty at a time and unions clusters across batches only when their canonical labels match exactly, so synonyms that land in different batches and get slightly different names survive as two one-instance concepts. That is also the cause of the one causal cycle in the graph (`graph_consistency.json`): three near-synonyms of self-explanation left separate. Normalisation now runs a guarded reconciliation pass afterwards — a researcher-declarable merge table in `config/concept_merges.yaml`, plus a subset rule that **refuses** to merge labels differing in polarity or degree, because "prior knowledge" and "low prior knowledge" are not the same concept.

**Richmond itself is not corrected, deliberately.** Re-running normalisation would renumber the graph every published figure rests on, so the fix prevents recurrence rather than rewriting the record. The declared merges for Richmond are written down in the config and marked inert.

**The four `role` verdicts are the ones worth a human's attention.** Two are labels that contradict their own quotes, which is the same class of error as the verification matcher defect found in November — a label asserting one polarity over evidence showing the other. Two are entities filed under the wrong role, including a moderation finding filed as an outcome when it is a context condition. None of the four would be caught by any count; each needed its quote read.

**What would make this a measurement rather than a triage:** a second coder, preferably human, on the same 79 with the same five categories, and a positive specific agreement between the two. That is the method this project already used for the gold standard, and it costs time rather than money.
