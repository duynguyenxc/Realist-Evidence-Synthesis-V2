Agentic realist evidence synthesis · verification report

Can a machine reproduce a human realist review?

Richmond et al. (2020) reviewed 28 studies on teaching clinical reasoning and concluded that the student is key. We gave our multi-agent system the same 28 papers. This page shows what it produced, and lays it beside the human review so you can judge the answer for yourself.

01The one-minute answer

Richmond's five reviewers spent months producing a realist review of 28 studies. Our system was given the same 28 studies and asked to do the same analytic work. Here is what each side concluded, in their own words.

Richmond et al. (2020) — five human reviewers

The student is key. Educational interventions "do not work independently, but work inter-dependently with the individual, their pre-existing clinical domain-specific knowledge and their ability to cope." The same intervention helps some students and harms others; students with low knowledge determine most whether an intervention succeeds.

Conclusions, p.717; Discussion, pp.716–717.

Our system — unaided, from the same 28 papers

Across the evidence pack, educational interventions develop analytical and non-analytical clinical reasoning less by teaching a single superior reasoning mode than by creating a fit between student context, case demands, and instructional design. Productive pathways recur when case-based practice, contrastive examples, schemas, prompts, model explanations, and feedback make diagnostic relations visible and usable. These resources trigger Structured Diagnostic Knowledge Building, Guided Self-Explanation, Structured reasoning uptake, and sometimes Pressure-driven case patterning, leading to improved diagnostic accuracy, reasoning-process development, knowledge retention, or Error-Resistant Dia…

Programme theory version 10, generated from 91 extracted configurations.

The short verdict. The system independently arrives at Richmond's central claim — that the same teaching resource helps or hinders depending on the student's prior knowledge — and it recovers all 28 studies, 92.8% of its claims resolve to a verbatim span in the source, and it recovers 29 of 40 of Richmond's causal links with at least one endpoint pinned to the matching concept. It does not recover Richmond's two emotion/coping contexts, it reconstructs 38 of Richmond's 46 named concepts in the same realist role, and it recovers none of the 40 causal links at the strictest exact-concept-pair level. Sections 4 and 5 show exactly how each of those numbers was produced, with its confidence interval and who judged it.

A CMOC — Context–Mechanism–Outcome configuration — is the unit of a realist review. It is one sentence of the form "for this kind of student, this teaching triggers this response, producing this result." Richmond's team wrote theirs by hand; ours were extracted by the system, one or more per paper, each carrying the verbatim sentence it came from. 26 of the 28 papers yielded at least one; the remaining papers are abstract-only records too short to support a configuration, and the system correctly declines to invent one.

High domain knowledge

Recovered (different studies) Richmond E03 · Results 3.2, pp.712-714

What Richmond concluded

students with high clinical domain-specific knowledge

How to read the two "Not recovered" blocks. Richmond's third and fourth contexts are about emotion — coping, self-confidence, self-efficacy. Our system found almost nothing for them, and that is the honest and correct result: those constructs are close to absent from the text of the 28 papers we hold. Richmond's reviewers brought them in from the wider simulation and emotion literature, which is a legitimate human move a text-grounded extractor is designed not to make. Forcing the system to produce them would mean letting it invent claims, which is exactly what the verbatim-quote requirement exists to prevent.

The one distinction to carry away. Five of these eight rows are deterministic: same inputs, same number, forever, no model involved.
