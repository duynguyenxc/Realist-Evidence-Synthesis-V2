Agentic realist evidence synthesis · verification report

Can a machine reproduce a human realist review?

Richmond et al. (2020) reviewed 28 studies on teaching clinical reasoning and concluded that the student is key. We gave our multi-agent system the same 28 papers. This page shows what it produced, and lays it beside the human review so you can judge the answer for yourself.

01The one-minute answer

Richmond's five reviewers spent months producing a realist review of 28 studies. Our system was given the same 28 studies and asked to do the same analytic work. Here is what each side concluded, in their own words.

Richmond et al. (2020) — five human reviewers

The student is key. Educational interventions "do not work independently, but work inter-dependently with the individual, their pre-existing clinical domain-specific knowledge and their ability to cope." The same intervention helps some students and harms others; students with low knowledge determine most whether an intervention succeeds.

Conclusions, p.717; Discussion, pp.716–717.

Our system — unaided, from the same 28 papers

Across the accumulated evidence, the developing programme theory is that educational interventions do not improve analytical or non-analytical clinical reasoning simply by teaching one preferred reasoning mode. Effects are generated when resources make diagnostic structure visible at a level students can use: contrastive cases, worked or annotated examples, self-explanation prompts, simulations, feedback, and repeated case exposure can trigger Structured reasoning support, Self-explanation for diagnostic checking, Guided reasoning uptake, Feedback-mediated reasoning change, or Voluntary formative retrieval. These responses are conditioned chiefly by students’ prior knowledge, illness-script …

Programme theory version 11, generated from 91 extracted configurations.

The short verdict. The system independently arrives at Richmond's central claim — that the same teaching resource helps or hinders depending on the student's prior knowledge — and it recovers all 28 studies, 95.6% of its claims resolve to a verbatim span in the source, and it recovers 32 of 40 of Richmond's causal links with at least one endpoint pinned to the matching concept. It does not recover Richmond's two emotion/coping contexts, it reconstructs 37 of Richmond's 46 named concepts in the same realist role, and it recovers none of the 40 causal links at the strictest exact-concept-pair level. Sections 4 and 5 show exactly how each of those numbers was produced, with its confidence interval and who judged it.

A CMOC — Context–Mechanism–Outcome configuration — is the unit of a realist review. It is one sentence of the form "for this kind of student, this teaching triggers this response, producing this result." Richmond's team wrote theirs by hand; ours were extracted by the system, one or more per paper, each carrying the verbatim sentence it came from. 26 of the 28 papers yielded at least one; the remaining papers are abstract-only records too short to support a configuration, and the system correctly declines to invent one.

What Richmond concluded

students with 'low knowledge,' low clinical domain-specific knowledge, or an inability to use knowledge in a reasoning situation

When students have low knowledge, low domain-specific knowledge or an inability to apply their knowledge in a reasoning situation (C), there are multiple ways (Mresource) in which educational interventions may develop their diagnostic accuracy or reasoning ability (O). High domain knowledge

students with high clinical domain-specific knowledge

students with different levels of knowledge within a group

When teaching students with different levels of pre-existing knowledge in a group (C), using strategies that promote knowledge retention (Mresource) will build upon what they already know (Mresponse) and develop understanding (Mresponse) regardless of their pre-existing knowledge level, which leads to increased learning (O) and further engagement (O).*

How to read the two "Not recovered" blocks. Richmond's third and fourth contexts are about emotion — coping, self-confidence, self-efficacy. Our system found almost nothing for them, and that is the honest and correct result: those constructs are close to absent from the text of the 28 papers we hold. Richmond's reviewers brought them in from the wider simulation and emotion literature, which is a legitimate human move a text-grounded extractor is designed not to make. Forcing the system to produce them would mean letting it invent claims, which is exactly what the verbatim-quote requirement exists to prevent.

The one distinction to carry away. Five of these eight rows are deterministic: same inputs, same number, forever, no model involved. Three are model-judged: a language model decides whether two differently-worded ideas mean the same thing, so they move between runs and between judges.
