1THE ANSWER — what Richmond concluded, what we concluded

What this section is: the conclusion each side reached, in its own words. Richmond's five reviewers on the left, our system on the right.

Richmond et al. (2020) — five human reviewers

The student is key. Educational interventions "do not work independently, but work inter-dependently with the individual, their pre-existing clinical domain-specific knowledge and their ability to cope." The same intervention helps some students and harms others; students with low knowledge determine most whether an intervention succeeds.

Conclusions, p.717; Discussion, pp.716–717.

Our system — unaided, from the same 28 papers

Across the accumulated evidence, undergraduate clinical-reasoning interventions appear to work less through a simple analytic-versus-non-analytic superiority effect than through the fit between learner knowledge, case/task demand, and instructional support. Case-based exposure, examples, feedback, self-explanation, contrast, and explicit reasoning instruction become generative when they help students build usable schemas, recognise diagnostically relevant features, compare alternatives, revise errors, and calibrate when to trust or check first impressions. The same resources can fail or backfire when learners lack prerequisite illness-script knowledge, when prompts are ambiguous or too deman…

Programme theory version 12, generated from 94 extracted configurations.

The short verdict. The system independently arrives at Richmond's central claim — that the same teaching resource helps or hinders depending on the student's prior knowledge — and it recovers all 28 studies, 93.1% of its claims resolve to a verbatim span in the source, and it recovers 29 of 40 of Richmond's causal links with at least one endpoint pinned to the matching concept. It does not recover Richmond's two emotion/coping contexts, it reconstructs 34 of Richmond's 46 named concepts in the same realist role, and it recovers none of the 40 causal links at the strictest exact-concept-pair level. Sections 4 and 5 show exactly how each of those numbers was produced, with its confidence interval and who judged it.

A CMOC — Context–Mechanism–Outcome configuration — is the unit of a realist review. It is one sentence of the form "for this kind of student, this teaching triggers this response, producing this result." Richmond's team wrote theirs by hand; ours were extracted by the system, one or more per paper, each carrying the verbatim sentence it came from. 26 of the 28 papers yielded at least one; the remaining papers are abstract-only records too short to support a configuration, and the system correctly declines to invent one.

How to read the two "Not recovered" blocks. Richmond's third and fourth contexts are about emotion — coping, self-confidence, self-efficacy. Our system found almost nothing for them, and that is the honest and correct result: those constructs are close to absent from the text of the 28 papers we hold. Richmond's reviewers brought them in from the wider simulation and emotion literature, which is a legitimate human move a text-grounded extractor is designed not to make. Forcing the system to produce them would mean letting it invent claims, which is exactly what the verbatim-quote requirement exists to prevent.

7LIMITS — what is NOT verified yet

What this section is: everything we have NOT yet proved, listed by us rather than found by a reviewer.

  1. No human coder has checked the extraction. The per-paper reference coding was produced by a different model family, blind — honest, but it is not a human gold standard. Two human coders and a Cohen's κ are the required next step; the blank worksheets already exist.

  4. Strict relation recall is zero. The causal shape is recovered; the exact concept-pair reconstruction is not. This is the real open problem and is stated as such.
  5. Precision was tested against a distractor pool we built ourselves, because Richmond did not publish their excluded studies. It shows the system discriminates; it is not a match to Richmond.
